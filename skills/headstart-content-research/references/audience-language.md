# Audience-Language Research

## Purpose And Boundary

For a family Resource, learn how parents frame the approved question before translating professional
sources into a writer brief. Public parent discussions can reveal vocabulary and points of
confusion; they do not establish clinical facts, coverage rules, prevalence, or what every family
feels. This is a bounded editorial observation pass, not a patient-data collection workflow.

Use existing authorized search or page-reading capabilities. Do not build a scraper, join a private
group, contact participants, or collect a broad corpus merely to complete this step. Follow the
active workspace's privacy and access rules; minimize retrieval and avoid identifying care stories.
Non-identifying question headings or a sanitized view can be sufficient. Never copy names, handles,
child details, or personal histories into the packet, shared fixtures, or article.

## Method

1. Search for public discussions that match the approved audience, topic, country, and decision.
   Inspect a small, diverse set where permitted; stop when more reads would not change the brief.
   Distinguish an actual parent question from a provider, marketing, bot, or professional post.
2. Record generic questions, unfamiliar terms, practical concerns, and useful distinctions in your
   own words. For example, a coverage question may also involve an unexpected bill, finding a
   practice that takes the exact plan, or whether it has openings. Do not infer frequency from a
   convenience sample or assign these concerns to all parents.
3. Separate what the audience asks from the answer. Verify any answer about health, insurance,
   laws, costs, availability, or Headstart's process through primary or approved sources. A forum
   anecdote never becomes a supported claim because several posts repeat it.
4. Translate the observations into brief decisions: what needs explanation before a task, an
   ordinary-language question, a term requiring introduction, or an example the writer can use
   once its facts are supported. Preserve adult dignity; do not imitate slang, grammatical errors,
   distress, or distinctive personal expression.
5. Distinguish research sources from reader links. A provider toolkit or regulatory source may
   support the internal ledger without being useful reading for a parent. Recommend a public link
   only when its audience, content, access, and reader purpose fit the article.

## Packet Record

Keep a compact audience-language section containing:

- source URLs or stable IDs, retrieval date, source type, and observed surface (full permitted
  discussion, non-identifying headings, sanitized excerpt, or supplied approved research);
- paraphrased questions or vocabulary observations, with source locators;
- writer implications and their rationale, clearly marked as editorial interpretation;
- the distinction from authoritative claim evidence; and
- sampling, access, recency, geographic, and representativeness limits.

Use `audience observation` as the ledger source role for these directions. Do not include raw posts
or long quotations. Carry useful paraphrases into the frozen packet, not a browsing transcript.

## Missing Or Limited Evidence

Record a failed read or metadata-only scope honestly. Do not say a forum was read when only its title
or search result was available. Use other permitted audience evidence, such as approved reviewer
feedback or public family-facing questions, and label the substitution. If the brief depends on an
unverified audience assumption, flag it for review rather than inventing a finding. An unavailable
forum does not block unrelated authoritative research or create permission to access private data.

Content Engine maintainers own this procedure. Refresh the topic-specific observations for each
new packet; do not install a permanent forum-derived vocabulary list or treat past examples as
current evidence for a new audience or topic.
