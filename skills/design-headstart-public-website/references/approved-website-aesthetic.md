## Visual North Star

Headstart's public website should feel:

> Human, photographic, editorial, warm, spacious, and confident.

The site is about people and relationships, not features and components. Real people should serve
as the visual identity of the brand, and photography should make the adjacent message tangible.

## Photography

Prioritize approved imagery of:

- real families, BCBAs, RBTs, and Headstart team members;
- people interacting or doing the actual work;
- candid conversations and collaborative moments;
- a BCBA and RBT reviewing or discussing a case;
- a family talking with someone; and
- a provider working with a child in a natural setting.

Avoid:

- abstract wellness stock photography;
- empty offices and generic hands-on-laptop images;
- AI illustrations or generated photography unless explicitly approved;
- decorative imagery unrelated to the copy; and
- crops where the human relationship, face, or relevant action is lost.

Use multiple meaningful photographic moments on long pages. A hero followed by an entire page of
UI blocks is not enough.

## Preferred Layouts

### Full-Bleed Photographic Hero

Use for major audience and campaign pages. Favor a large image, readable contrast, left-aligned
copy, a coral primary action, and an optional subordinate secondary action.

### Photo And Text Split

Use for who Headstart is, how Headstart helps, care philosophy, insurance help, human support,
mentorship, and professional relationships.

### Alternating Process Rows

Use for a process that benefits from human context. Alternate image and text placement to create
rhythm while preserving a clear step sequence.

### Editorial Testimonials

Feature one strong quote with real attribution and a photo when available. Supporting quotes may
sit nearby with lighter treatment. Do not default every quote to an identical bordered card.

## Component Selection

| Content                            | Preferred treatment                              |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Main promise                       | Full-bleed photo hero                            |
| Who Headstart is or why it matters | Photo and text split                             |
| Process                            | Alternating photo steps or clean numbered steps  |
| Abstract benefits                  | Open text hierarchy rather than cards by default |
| One meaningful metric              | Large standalone stat                            |
| Insurance brands                   | Horizontal logo band                             |
| Testimonial                        | Editorial quote with real attribution and photo  |
| Jobs                               | Cards or rows with real role details             |
| Articles and resources             | Editorial image cards                            |
| Providers                          | Profile cards with approved photos               |
| Final action                       | Photo-led or strong full-width color block       |
| Contact path                       | Small utility strip or simple action             |

Cards are for distinct objects. They are not the default container for abstract values such as
support, quality, collaboration, guidance, flexibility, or care.

## Color And Type

Use the established palette as hierarchy:

- **dark teal:** strong full-width moments, professional sections, and final actions;
- **white:** primary breathing room and clarity;
- **warm cream or off-white:** stories, testimonials, and editorial relief;
- **pale mint:** occasional utility or reassuring moments;
- **coral:** primary actions; and
- **teal:** links, small labels, and limited emphasis.

Do not highlight every keyword or alternate every section with pale mint.

Typography should reduce the need for containers. Use strong but restrained hierarchy, readable
body measures, and short page-level headings. Eyebrows are small labels used sparingly, not a
required companion to every heading.

Directional desktop ranges from the approved source are 48-64px for a hero H1, 40-48px for a
section H2, 22-28px for a subsection H3, and 18-20px for major landing-page body copy. The active
design system remains authoritative when its tokens differ.

## Page Rhythm

Strong public pages change shape as the reader scrolls. A useful sequence might be:

> Full-bleed photo, photo/text split, open process, dark section, editorial testimonial,
> transactional content, photographic final action.

Avoid repeating white card grid, pale card grid, white card grid. Whitespace should create
hierarchy and warmth without making related sections feel disconnected.

## Calls To Action

Name real actions, such as:

- Get Started
- Get Started with ABA
- Find a Provider
- Browse Providers
- View Open Roles
- Text Our RBT Team
- Book a Fit Call
- Explore Family Resources
- See How It Works

Challenge vague labels such as `Discover`, `Explore Solutions`, `Unlock More`, `Start Your
Journey`, and repeated `Learn More` buttons. Supporting copy should reduce uncertainty about what
happens after the action.

## Testimonials And Proof

Use testimonials to prove a specific promise:

- family stories may demonstrate responsiveness, communication, getting started, provider
  relationships, or progress the family can describe;
- RBT stories may demonstrate BCBA availability, listening, hands-on help, mentorship,
  communication, or feeling valued; and
- BCBA stories may demonstrate ownership, autonomy, operational support, confidence starting a
  practice, or time returned to clinical work.

Use stats only when they make a meaningful point. One large number with a plain-language
explanation is usually stronger than a decorative metric dashboard.

## Generic-Template Warning Signs

Reconsider a page when several of these are true:

- centered headings repeatedly introduce identical card grids;
- generic line icons carry the section's visual identity;
- every idea has a border or rounded container;
- pale mint appears on every other section;
- decorative gradients or blobs fill empty space;
- the same three-column feature grid appears more than once;
- a carousel replaces a clearer static editorial treatment;
- the page has more icons than meaningful faces;
- the page is clean but emotionally empty; or
- the design could be relabeled for any healthcare or software company without changing its form.

## Visual Review

Before shipping, inspect the actual page at representative mobile, tablet, desktop, and wide
desktop sizes. Check image crops, faces, names, controls, heading wraps, line lengths, spacing,
alignment, hover and focus states, card consistency, mobile sequencing, empty areas, and section
transitions.

The page passes only when it remains coherent and recognizably Headstart across the supported
viewports, not merely when the component code is valid.
