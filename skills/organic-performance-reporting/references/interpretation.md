# Agent-led interpretation and targeted research

The engine makes selected evidence reproducible. It does not decide which explanation is true,
which initiative matters, or which further evidence is worth obtaining. The reporting agent owns
that investigation within the user's scope. Business definitions and consequential writes retain
their normal approval boundaries.

## Investigate the question, not a predetermined story

Start with the verified baseline and its coverage/quality notes. Identify the movements material to
the report's decision. Form plausible competing explanations; a decline, improvement, or flat
result need not have a single identifiable cause. Decide what observation would strengthen or
weaken each explanation before searching for support.

Choose targeted follow-up reads when they could change the conclusion: affected versus unchanged
page/query cohorts, earlier trajectories, country/device differences, instrumentation changes,
release and deployment evidence, or third-party demand context. Use the existing read capabilities;
do not require a new engine feature for every exploratory slice. Persist query definitions,
source dates, identifiers, quality notes, and any deterministic calculations used in the report.
Label exploratory cohorts separately; never silently revise canonical audience or pillar mappings.

Revise, combine, or reject hypotheses as evidence arrives. Keep a compact record of material
alternatives and why they were retained or rejected. A well-supported conclusion of insufficient
evidence, no meaningful change, or no recommended action is a successful result.

Before deeper investigation, choose a proportionate time/tool-call budget for this report and
record it with the evidence packet. Stop when the decision is adequately supported, the next read
is unlikely to change it, access is unavailable, or the budget is exhausted. Repeated reads without
new evidence are not progress. Report unresolved uncertainty; do not continue an unbounded search
or ask for unrelated permissions merely to fill a report section.

## Use web research when it can improve interpretation

Use available search, browsing, or research capabilities when the mechanism, expected timing,
measurement semantics, or relevant industry context is uncertain and material. Research is not
mandatory for every initiative, and no particular vendor or MCP is required. If access is absent,
narrow the claim and describe the gap rather than supplying an invented benchmark.

Frame a specific question tied to the actual change and observed signal. For example, after a batch
metadata release, investigate how the changed fields are used, when crawling/reprocessing might
make them visible, and what evidence could distinguish search appearance or CTR changes from wider
demand, ranking, or tracking changes. Titles, meta descriptions, canonicals, and content edits are
not interchangeable interventions. Do not assume impressions, rankings, clicks, and GA4 sessions
must improve together or follow one universal sequence or timetable.

Prefer official platform documentation for platform behavior and original studies or experiments
for empirical benchmarks. Read the supporting source, not just a search snippet or generated
research summary. Check its date, population, intervention, measurement method, and applicability.
Distinguish a formal standard, documented behavior, study result, and practitioner opinion. A
plausible explanation does not become an industry standard through repetition.

Retain the URL, relevant section, publication/update date when available, retrieval date, claim
supported, applicability limits, and how the evidence changed the interpretation. Seek contrary
evidence when a material conclusion depends on a disputed claim. Keep general background research
separate from first-party observations and verified deployment dates. Later research may inform a
retrospective explanation but must not introduce post-report site performance into an as-of report.
Do not send credentials, private source artifacts, PHI, or internal-only identifiers in web queries.

Use research to calibrate plausible timing, confidence, alternatives, or the next measurement—not
to allocate a percentage of observed growth to a release or to guarantee a benefit. Do not convert
a case study's timing into a hard-coded success/failure deadline. If independent causal evidence is
absent, describe a possible contribution and the evidence limits rather than claiming causation.

## Example source roles, not a fixed impact model

These source sections were checked on 2026-09-10 as examples of research relevant to metadata work:

- [Google title links: how titles are created](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link): recrawling/reprocessing title sources can take days to weeks. That is not a promised traffic or ranking improvement window.
- [Google snippets: how snippets are created](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet): snippets can come from page content or a meta description and vary by query; a metadata deployment does not establish that the new text was shown.
- [Google traffic-drop analysis](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops): page/query patterns and wider demand context help distinguish competing explanations; apply the reasoning to gains as well as declines.

The reporting workflow owner maintains this reference. Recheck the relevant sections when using
them for a material current claim or when their content changes; cite the retrieved version in the
report evidence packet. These examples deliberately do not define an expected uplift, a metric
sequence, or a universal time-to-effect. Follow the separate
[historical-context policy](historical-context.md) for the approved lookback and initiative register.

## Launch context is evidence for judgment, not generated editorial copy

Reassess initiative call-outs for every reporting period. The July and August 2026 workbooks are
pre-architecture-launch examples, not reusable conclusions that the architecture is still pending.
The engine can calculate dated baseline/comparison eligibility; it must not decide the narrative,
which initiative deserves attention, whether an effect has matured, or whether an initiative worked.

Use verified deployment and measurement dates, observed page/query trends, migration mappings,
earlier initiatives, and relevant research to decide what deserves explanation. Keep these questions
separate: did a change reach production, is a comparison arithmetically valid, and what explanation
does the evidence support? A first complete month answers neither maturity nor causality by itself.

For a newly launched section, preserve pre-launch absence and annotate the partial launch month.
Partial-month observations may be useful and should not be erased merely because the standard
full-month MoM panel is not yet eligible. The agent may investigate a separately labeled matched-day
or migrated-content cohort when useful, recording its dates, mapping, calculation, rationale, and
limits. Do not silently replace the canonical scorecard comparison with that diagnostic.

Existing pages, established audience journeys, and earlier technical/metadata initiatives can have
meaningful pre-launch histories. Do not reset them all to the site's architecture launch date.
For migrated articles or locations, decide whether an old/new URL mapping supports a like-for-like
diagnostic; retain the observed-route series and disclose composition changes. Verify audience,
topic, and collection metadata rather than guessing a flat Resource URL's category from its slug.

Write the resulting baseline, timing, migration, and contribution call-outs yourself in the
narrative bindings. Do not mechanically translate lifecycle status codes into stakeholder prose,
repeat last month's caveat, or describe every new section as a success once a date threshold passes.

## Handoff

For each material conclusion provide the observed movement, interpretation and confidence,
supporting source identifiers, meaningful alternatives, and next action or measurement if warranted.
Connect externally researched guidance to the specific initiative only to the extent the evidence
supports. Keep claims observed, inferred, or recommended; do not force an action count or a win.
