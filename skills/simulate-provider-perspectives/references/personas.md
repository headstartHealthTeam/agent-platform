# Provider Persona Model

Version: 0.1
Last calibrated: 2026-07-21
Status: Initial evidence-backed model

These are composite operating profiles, not real people. Select a foundational persona, then add behavioral overlays, operating scenarios, and usage context. Do not turn an overlay into a permanent identity.

## Shared Provider Jobs

The following needs recur across practice stages and should be tested for every persona:

1. Financial health and visibility: reimbursement timing, expected payout, revenue leakage, cancellations, loan repayment, and whether completed work became cash.
2. Intake and growth velocity: qualified client pipeline, authorization status and blockers, staffing capacity, and utilization of authorized hours.
3. Autonomy with support: visibility into Headstart's work, the ability to make clinical and business decisions, and meaningful opt-outs.
4. Operational and clinical safeguards: supervision compliance, documentation, staffing continuity, and measurable client progress.

## Foundational Personas

### P1 — Transitional Practice Launcher

Stable profile:

- Building a Headstart-supported practice while retaining another job, contract, or income source.
- Early in Headstart tenure and business ownership.
- Stronger clinical knowledge than operational familiarity.
- Limited and sometimes fragmented time for administrative work.

Primary priorities:

- Understand the sequence and prerequisites.
- See credible progress toward a viable first caseload.
- Complete tasks quickly without learning every connected system at once.

Intake perspective:

- Treats the page partly as an instructional guide.
- Needs a clear next action, effort estimate, deadline, and explanation of dependencies.
- Benefits from checklists, examples, saved progress, reminders, and resumable tasks.

Sensitive failures:

- Unexplained terminology or hidden prerequisites.
- Long tasks that cannot be paused.
- Notifications that assume frequent portal visits.
- Delays interpreted as personal failure rather than a documented dependency.

Do not assume:

- Part-time means uncommitted.
- Limited availability is caused only by another job.

### P2 — All-In New Solo Owner

Stable profile:

- Headstart represents a primary professional and financial commitment.
- Early-to-middle Headstart tenure and rapidly learning operations.
- Personally performs most clinical, intake, staffing, and administrative work.
- Each client admission and payment materially affects planning.

Primary priorities:

- Predict cash flow and intake timing.
- Know who owns each blocker and when movement is expected.
- Build confidence that Headstart is actively advancing the work.

Intake perspective:

- Treats the page as the source of truth for business momentum.
- Wants qualified status detail without being exposed to unreliable early pipeline noise.
- Needs explicit ownership, timing, milestones, and intervention thresholds.

Sensitive failures:

- Generic “in progress” states.
- Statuses that disagree across tools or do not explain the source.
- Missing authorization detail.
- Celebratory or optimistic language that overpromises readiness.

Do not assume:

- Financial sensitivity means lack of clinical focus.
- Repeated status checks always mean low tool fluency.

### P3 — Autonomy-First Established Provider

Stable profile:

- Established solo or small-practice clinician with greater Headstart and operating experience.
- Has a preferred approach to scheduling, client fit, family communication, and staffing.
- May use Headstart for some or all of the practice.
- Values accurate information and professional discretion over step-by-step instruction.

Primary priorities:

- Preserve decision rights while reducing administrative burden.
- Evaluate client, staff, geographic, and schedule fit.
- Access exceptions and opt-outs without escalation.

Intake perspective:

- Treats the page as decision support, not a script.
- Wants concise detail, control over scheduling, and reversible acceptance decisions.
- Expects advanced paths to coexist with sensible defaults.

Sensitive failures:

- Forced scheduling or acceptance.
- Prescriptive guidance with no dismissal or advanced path.
- Hiding clinically relevant match information.
- Treating deliberate selectivity as inactivity.

Do not assume:

- Longer tenure means comfort with every tool.
- Autonomy preference means resistance to support.

### P4 — Growth-Constrained Practice Builder

Stable profile:

- Manages several clients and a growing RBT or BCBA team.
- Balances clinical delivery with recruiting, supervision, payroll, and practice operations.
- Delegates some work but remains involved in exceptions.
- Staffing and operational capacity increasingly constrain growth.

Primary priorities:

- Identify where capacity, authorized hours, and revenue are being lost.
- Coordinate ownership across staff and Headstart.
- Keep staffing, intake, supervision, and scheduling synchronized.

Intake perspective:

- Treats the page as a working queue across several clients.
- Needs portfolio views, assignments, filters, deadlines, and exception visibility.
- Wants to distinguish family, provider, staff, payer, and Headstart blockers.

Sensitive failures:

- A purely client-by-client experience with no aggregate view.
- Ambiguous rendering provider or assignee.
- No delegation, bulk, or staff-access model.
- Staffing changes that invalidate schedules without warning.

Do not assume:

- The owner personally completes every task.
- More clients imply standardized clinical or scheduling preferences.

### P5 — Value-Scrutinizing Practice Operator

Stable profile:

- Runs a larger, multi-site, multi-market, or substantially delegated practice.
- Has higher operational sophistication and established internal processes.
- May rely on administrators or clinical directors to use the portal.
- Evaluates Headstart as an operating partner and cost center.

Primary priorities:

- Verify that Headstart's contribution justifies its take rate.
- Measure financial performance, utilization, staffing, compliance, and outcomes.
- Assign accountability and manage exceptions without entering every case.

Intake perspective:

- Treats the page as part of an operating system.
- Needs role-based access, portfolio comparison, auditability, integrations, and clear service ownership.
- Looks for evidence of work completed behind the scenes.

Sensitive failures:

- Requiring owner-only actions.
- No role, permission, or delegation model.
- Inability to distinguish Headstart work from practice work.
- Metrics without drill-down, provenance, or actionability.

Do not assume:

- Sophistication removes the need for onboarding when workflows change.
- A larger practice values speed over clinical outcomes.

## Behavioral Overlays

Apply an overlay only when the scenario or evidence supports it.

- Guidance-needing: lacks the process model or terminology and needs examples or sequencing.
- Status-anxious: understands the goal but lacks confidence in ownership, timing, or whether work is progressing.
- Tool-fragmented: can operate individual systems but struggles with inconsistent data or navigation across them.
- Capacity-constrained: has limited attention because of workload, health, caregiving, travel, staffing disruption, or another temporary constraint.
- Fit-first: prioritizes clinical, family, staff, geographic, or schedule fit over intake speed.
- Mobile-interrupted: uses the experience in short bursts between sessions or meetings.
- Exception-heavy: manages unusual payer, staffing, custody, authorization, or clinical circumstances.

## Operating Scenarios

Use one or more scenarios to stress the design:

- First prospective client.
- Initial consultation scheduling.
- Assessment delayed by family documentation.
- Initial or treatment authorization delayed, denied, or returned for revision.
- Mixed actions owned by different parties.
- RBT candidate review or staffing loss.
- Accepted client later determined to be a poor fit.
- Authorized hours cannot be fully staffed or scheduled.
- Multiple clients reach the same deadline.
- Provider or delegate takes over work started by someone else.

## Usage Contexts

- Mobile between sessions.
- Desktop during weekly planning.
- Returning from a notification or direct link.
- In a meeting with a family or staff member.
- Reviewing a portfolio during a staffing or revenue incident.
- Returning after several days away from the portal.

## Selection Rules

- Evaluate all five personas for broad provider-portal work.
- Narrow the set only when the target audience is explicitly constrained.
- If the same finding applies to everyone, report it as a shared provider need rather than inventing five phrasings.
- If two personas disagree, preserve the conflict and recommend progressive disclosure, defaults plus opt-outs, roles, or configurable notifications.
